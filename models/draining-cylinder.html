The model

Let y be the water depth, u the speed at which the surface falls and V the jet speed at the hole. The tank area is A = πR² and the effective hole area is a = Cdπr². Write k = A/a.

  1. Conservation of mass: what leaves the surface leaves the hole.
    A u = a V   ⇒   V = k u
  2. Unsteady Bernoulli along the water column. The column of height y must be accelerated, so the familiar Torricelli balance gains an inertia term.
    y dudt = g y − 12(V² − u²)  =  g y − k² − 12 u²
  3. Using dy/dt = −u and starting from rest gives the speed at every depth exactly.
    u² = 2 g yk² − 2 [ 1 − (y/h)k² − 2 ]
  4. The time to empty is T = ∫0h dy/u, which comes out as a Beta function. The page evaluates it numerically, so every value of k ≥ 1 works.
    T = h2 g (k² − 2)½ · B( 12(k² − 2), 12 )

Water drained over time

The volume that has left the cylinder by time t is whatever the surface has dropped through, and its rate is the flow through the hole.

Vout(t) = πR² [ h − y(t) ]     Q(t) = dVoutdt = a V(t)

For a small hole the depth follows y = h(1 − t/T)², so the drained volume is Vout = πR²h[1 − (1 − t/T)²]. The flow is fastest at the start and tapers to zero, so half the water is gone after only about 29% of the draining time. Water released at time τ with jet speed V reaches the ground a fall time later, found from H = VΔt + ½gΔt², which is why the ground curve lags behind.

What the answer reduces to

Small hole (r ≪ R). The inertia term dies away almost at once and the result is Torricelli's law, T ≈ (R/r)²√(2h/g) for an ideal hole. Halving the hole radius makes the tank take four times as long.

Hole radius equal to R. With an open base k = 1, the equation becomes du/dt = g, and the water simply drops out as a slug in T = √(2h/g). The quasi-steady formula would wrongly give zero here, which is why the inertia term is kept.

Height above ground, H. H has no effect on how long the cylinder takes to empty, because the pressure at the hole is atmospheric whatever the height. It only sets how long the stream takes to fall and how hard it lands: the last water reaches the ground about √(2H/g) after the tank empties.

Assumptions: open top, frictionless flow apart from Cd, no surface tension or vortex at the drain, and a thin base so the water in the hole itself has negligible inertia. The cylinder height L only matters as the upper limit on h.
